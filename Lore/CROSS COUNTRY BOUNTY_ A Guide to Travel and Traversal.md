#### **CROSS COUNTRY BOUNTY: A Guide to Travel and Traversal**

BASICS  
Unlike the campaigns we have done in the past, this one will involve traveling from one place to another. As bounty hunters, you will be tasked with following leads and chasing down your quarries across the new nation of Elustria. The map below is standard issue for all CROWN guild members (that’s you). 

*If you look closely, each road has a series of tick marks. These points represent a day’s worth of travel. Travel in this campaign will be measured by days, not distance. Each day the DM will ask one of you to roll a **D20**. This roll will determine what kind of **Road Scenario** you get.* 

**A Road Scenario** represents the one unique thing your party will see that day while crossing this island nation. There are three different types of Road Scenarios:

* **Combat Scenario**  
  * Example: bandits force you to pay a fee to cross a bridge, goblins waiting to ambush you  
      
*  **Info/Lore Scenario**  
  * Example: A lord has recently purchased some acres and is kicking out the original inhabitants, some shady guards are taking food from villagers as a “security fee”  
      
* **Tasks/Treasures Scenario**  
  * A botanist needs help reaching a flower in the nearby mountains, the party takes a break near a river and spots an old lock box hidden among the rocks

#### **THE PARTY’s TRAVEL PLANNING**

You guys will have to focus and keep track of two key elements when it comes to travel: **the pace at which you travel** and **rations.** Make sure to discuss these things amongst yourselves before embarking on these dangerous roads.

##### **PACE**

There are three different paces at which the party can travel: Slow, Moderate, and Fast. Each with their own strengths and weaknesses. Pace will determine a few things:

* Number of Days to Destination  
* Encounter Awareness  
* Ration Usage

These rules are homebrewed and are in no way perfect or finalized. The DM will be revising as we go, and all input and feedback is welcome.

PACE: Days to Destination  
The pace at which your team travels will obviously affect how many days it takes for you to go from one place to the next. **Moderate Pace** follows the tick marks shown on the map. If there are 9 tick marks between City A and City B, then at a *moderate pace* it would take you 9 days. Let’s keep this travel example to explain the **Slow and Fast Paces**. 

If your party decides to go at a **Fast Pace**, then you would skip every other tick mark. So instead of it taking 9 days, it takes your party 5 days. However, you still interact with all 9 Road Scenarios (essentially instead of one a day, you get two). *Don’t worry, this trade off will make sense as you continue*.

If your party decides to go at a **Slow Pace**, then you add a day every five tick marks. So in this example, a 9 day trip turns into 10 days. If there are 12 tick marks on the map, then at a Slow Pace it would take you 14 days ( 5(add 1\) \+ 5(add 1\) \+ 2 ).

##### **PACE: Encounter Awareness**

If the party rolls a Combat Scenario for their daily Road Scenario, the DM will ask you to roll **a percentage die aka D100**. When it comes to these kinds of scenarios, your party will be able to take one of four different actions based on your roll: Sneak by, attack first, roll for initiative, and get surprised. 

* **Sneak By**: Avoid the combat scenario entirely  
* **First To Attack**: Every member of your party gets 1 Turn before rolling initiative. They can place their characters wherever they want within reason (speak to DM first)  
* **Roll For Initiative**: Everyone rolls for their turn and combat begins  
* **Surprised**: The enemies all each get a turn before you roll for initiative

The table below shows you when your party can take one of those actions based on the roll you get.

| Pace (Travel Speed) | SNEAK BY | FIRST TO ATTACK | ROLL FOR INITIATIVE | SURPRISED |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Moderate | 1-25 | 1-40 | 41-75 | 76-100 |
| Slow | 1-40 | 1-50 | 51-85 | 86-100 |
| Fast | 1-10 | 1-15 | 16-70 | 71-100 |

For example, if a player rolls a **27** at a **Moderate Pace** then they just barely missed their chance to Sneak By and avoid the combat all together. However, a **27** at a **Slow Pace** would allow your party to skip the encounter by going around. And finally a **27** at a **Fast Pace** will have everyone start combat like normal. This D100 roll will also occur with a few select Road Scenarios from the other scenario lists.

##### **PACE: Rations**

Rations are the most important resource that your party will be tasked with keeping track of (with some oversight by the DM). While in cities and towns, each character has to have one ration a day (about a pound of food). However, this will change on the road and it will be dependent on how fast or slow you are traveling.

| Pace | Rations Per Person Per Day  |
| :---- | :---- |
| Slow | .5 |
| Moderate | 1  |
| Fast | 2  |

    
What happens if you run out of food while traveling? Well the rules on that will be tweaked slightly to make travel challenging without having to drag it out. Each character can go a certain amount of days without eating. This number is determined by this formula: 3 \+ (CON modifier / 2). You round up if you CON modifier is an odd number, and if you have a negative CON modifier you round down (CON \= \-3, then you add \-2). Once you go this many days without eating, **your character starts to starve**. Starvation is measured by **Levels of Exhaustion**. For each day you go without eating once you’ve reached your limit, you add a level. The chart below shows you what happens with each level. The exhaustion can only be taken away by eating and long resting.

A creature suffers the effect of its current level of exhaustion as well as all lower levels. Pace and rationing are very important things to keep track of if you want your party to make it safely to your destination. I want this to be challenging but not detrimental. It’s a way to ensure that the party can really take their time going from one major city to another; making it as big of a deal as it was actually traveling on foot back then. And generally I believe that if you are certain about staying the course when going from City A to City B, you will be able to manage. Sure there are a few encounters here and there that will throw a wrench in your planning (Oregon Trail-esq), but I believe it will not result in too much stress. 

That being said, these sentiments are different if we are talking about tracking a quarry.

#### **TRACKING A QUARRY**

This campaign is going to revolve around hunting for bounties to sustain your party’s adventures. To track down the big bounty that is starting your journey, you’re gonna need to make some money to travel so far. That’s where working for CROWN comes in handy. Every town and city will have a Job Board, and most will have WANTED posters that CROWN distributes based on what region those targets are located in. Following a quarry around Elustria has two phases: Before the quarry is aware of their tail, and after.

Let’s say your party gets a lead on a bounty; An NPC tells you that he saw the quarry traveling towards Drakenberg three days ago. If you decide to start going after them, there are a few things your party needs to take into account:

* Finding/Following Tracks (Nature and Survival Checks)  
* Flee Action (In-Combat Chase)  
* Pace Difference 

##### **FINDING/FOLLOWING TRACKS**

We’ll use the same example as before: the quarry is 3 days away in the direction of Drakenberg. Once your party knows which way to go on the map and sets off, one to two players can make a Nature/Survival Check after each Road Scenario. When you and your quarry are on the road, we are essentially considering the days between the two groups as spaces that need to be closed to reach one another. So in this case, your party would be three spaces away from reaching the quarry.   
It will depend on the situation, but usually your quarry will be traveling as well, meaning that they are moving a set amount of spaces away from you while you are trying to close that gap. This will vary based on the Pace Difference, but we’ll get into that later. For this example, let’s say that both you and the quarry are traveling at a Moderate Pace (**aka 1 space a day**). While you are on the road, you can close the gap by making Nature/Survival Checks as a way of tracking your quarry’s whereabouts. This chart below shows examples of what to expect based on your roll, and how many spaces you advance.  

| TRACKING CHECK | 1-5 | 6-10 | 11-15 | 16-20 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Nature** | The surrounding area seems undisturbed | The brush and undergrowth looks like it's been flattened. Could be the target or an animal  | You notice signs of foraging and find an old camp site. | You can see smoke off in the distance, or you hear a horse winnie from afar |
| **Survival** | Tracks seem to disappear, no sign of camps  | Tracks are faint (hard to tell at a fork in the road, but easy to keep up the pace if on a straight road) | Tracks are much clearer. You and the Party are able to follow them much faster (Forks in the road do not matter) | You come across an old campsite of your target’s. You can roll an Investigation Check on it |
| **SPACES** | **\-1** | **\+0** | **\+1** | **\+2** |

**TO BE CLEAR**, your party will always travel one space forward on the map. The different Space Additions you see above affects your quarry’s location, not yours. So if your party gets a \+1 on their roll, that three space gap has gone down to one. If your party gets a \+0, the gap is still at three, and if your party receives a \-1 then the gap grows to four. 

WHEN THE GAP IS CLOSED (**turns to 0**), your Road Scenario is confronting your quarry. 

##### **FLEE ACTION:**

Each quarry will be different from the last. Some are stubborn, confident, or prepared enough to fight your party when you catch them; Others, not so much. In the heat of battle some of your quarries might opt to run away from combat, sometimes even leaving their travel companions behind to buy them some time. Your party will have 4 turns to chase after him before he “disappears.” This entails at least one party member reaching the same side of the combat map that the quarry fled to before those 4 turns are up (this may vary from scenario to scenario at the DM’s discretion). If no one is able to reach that side of the map before the allotted amount of turns, your party will go back to tracking them on the map like normal. 

If someone does manage to chase after him, both the player and the quarry will engage in a series of **competing rolls via Athletics/Acrobatics checks**. The distance between you and the quarry is determined by the amount of turns the quarry was off the map (1 \- 4 spaces). So both you and the quarry will roll either of the checks, and whoever has the highest roll moves one space forward. This repeats itself until you have **either closed the gap or the quarry successfully makes it 7 spaces away from you**. If combat is still in progress, the rolls are taken on the runners’ respective turns that round. If you manage to close the gap between yourself and the quarry, you are now dueling them. If the other party members are done with their combat while you are still dueling, each turn of theirs gets them one space closer to where you guys are fighting.

##### **PACE DIFFERENCE:**  

Your quarry’s pace will change if they manage to escape combat (or for some other in-game reason at the DM’s discretion). Now they know they have a tail on them, so they might choose to go at a Fast Pace. This stage of tracking will involve an extra D20 roll to determine both of the parties’ space gain or loss. So the party will still roll their Nature/Survival Check, and if you manage to roll a 6 or higher then you get to roll for PACE DIFFERENCE as shown below.

| PACE DIFFERENCE | 1-5 | 6-10 | 11-15 | 16-20 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Slow | \-2 | \-1 | 0 | \+2 |
| Normal | \-1 | \+0 | \+1 | \+2 |
| Fast | \-2 | \+1 | \+2 | \+3 |

   
Not only would you receive the bonus from the Nature/Survival Check, you would also receive whatever additional bonus you roll from this chart based on the Pace your party is traveling at. The quarry will be rolling and moving according to this chart once they know they have someone chasing after them. If you catch up to them on the road, you confront the quarry and start combat. If they arrive at town or city, the amount of spaces between you and the quarry will determine how well they’ve blended in, or if they’ve already gotten what they needed and fled.

##### **IN CASE OF EMERGENCIES:**

**So what happens if you’re on a quarry’s tail and you roll poorly on your Nature/Survival Check?** Well, there are a few things you can do depending on where you find yourself. Let’s say your party was two spaces away from the quarry, and the **Nature Check Roll** ends up being a **3**; Your party can choose to either stay and check the same area tomorrow, or move on to the next camp site and check for tracks there.   
You will have to use your best judgment and knowledge of the quarry that you have to make this decision; especially if you find yourself at a fork in the road. Staying will obviously add a space between you and the quarry, but you also don’t want to end up going the wrong way just to make the gap bigger by accident. **This will greatly be affected by the different kinds of roads and trails that will be discussed later in greater detail.** 

You can also ask any NPCs you run into on a Road Scenario. You will have to persuade them to tell you. Depending on your Persuasion Check Roll, the NPC will give advantage on their Perception Roll. If they don’t roll well, the NPC will still point you in the right direction but due to the bad directions given it will add a space to the quarry’s distance. If you chase your quarry into the city, they will be assigned a “hiding spot” which the party will have to suss out via role playing.

#### **DIFFERENT PATHS: A Guide to Roads, Trails, and the Wilderness**

The standard issue map you receive from CROWN (above) only shows what’s referred to as the Royal Highway and the toll houses along the way. While traveling Elustria, you will come across roads and paths that you will mark on your map as you pass by (very dependent on Pace). Below you will see a breakdown of the map’s legend.

*Each road or path you take will affect what your party rolls for the Road Scenario of that day. Each area of the map has its own set of regional encounters. The DM will roll to determine what category your D20 scenario roll pulls from. When referring to advantage and disadvantage for these rolls, it is referring to which category to pick from. The DM will roll a 1d4, and that will determine the list.*  

Combat scenarios are favorable (for the DM) where as the other two categories are not. If the DM rolls at disadvantage and he gets two different numbers, the call goes to the option that will avoid combat scenarios if it can.  Below you have an explanation of each road:

1. **Royal Highway**: This (mostly) paved road is considered the safest way to travel around Elustria. The active guard patrols between most major cities and the quantity of people using this highway reduces the likelihood of having to fight or run away (DM rolls at disadvantage for Road Scenario). Only roads with toll houses.  
2. **Highway**: These somewhat kept roads are manable to navigate; Guarded infrequently enough that danger might just loom around the corner (DM rolls normal). These roads are commonly used and very well known to those of the entire region. When your party crosses them at a Fast Pace, these roads will partially be revealed.  
3. **Road**: These paths are rarely even loosely paved. They’re usually dirt tracks that part the way enough for the locals to venture through as short cuts made by their ancestors (DM rolls at advantage for Road Scenarios). When your party crosses them at a Moderate Pace, these roads will partially be revealed.  
4. **Trail**: These paths are only known by those who use it frequently; Poachers, hunters, bandits, smugglers, etc. Trails are dangerous because they cut through the Wilderness. Due to this, not only does the DM roll at advantage for the Road Encounter, the Wilderness Scenario List has more combat scenarios than the others; Some of which can interrupt your long rests. These paths are for professionals, or the desperate. No horses or carriages can use these roads. When your party crosses them at a Slow Pace, these roads will partially be revealed.  
5. **Toll House**: These safe havens are located only along the Royal Highway. If you decide to cross this place on your map, it will cost each player 50 gold to get by. This includes right of passage, as well as room and board at the Toll House where you are fed one High Quality Ration for dinner (more on that later). Evenings at the Toll House can be much longer than your normal Road Scenarios depending on who else is staying there and who may have passed through. Toll Houses can not have Combat Scenarios, and may even have multiple Task Scenarios.    
6. **Point of Interest**: These little spots sprinkled around the map are exactly as their name suggests. These places can range from bandit camps to buried treasure to ruins left behind from the Unifying War. These tend to only be discovered either by accident or from an NPC/quest line.

#### **TRAVELING THROUGH WILDERNESS**

Your party can decide to travel through the Wilderness in an attempt to take a short cut, or maybe cut off a quarry they know is going to a certain location. There will be a ruler at the bottom of the map that will show the party how many days it would take to cross that span of wilderness (normal maps usually have this kind of thing where 1 inch \= 500 miles). Cutting through Wilderness takes **at least one day of travel**, regardless of how short the distance is (**think of it as Difficult Terrain**). Going at a Slow Pace automatically adds an extra day to your Wilderness travel. 

Most, if not all, of your road encounters in the Wilderness will be combat, and night time disruptions are more common. Different patches of Wilderness will have certain level caps to them. For example, one area of the nation could have Level 5 monsters, but another may have Level 15 monsters. Be cautious and make sure to ask the locals about the area before you venture out. **Think of the Wilderness as a Boss Rush; You will most likely not get a decent long rest until you are back on the road.**

| Type | Price | Starvation Effects | Nature/Survival Checks | Pace Differences |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |

#### **SPLITTING UP THE PARTY**

The DM will be keeping a timer for each group of PCs which will change depending on how many are in that group. Once that timer is up, the group can decide on whether they’d like some additional time to WRAP UP the interaction they are in, or they can opt to wait through the other group(s) turn to continue the conversation with more time.
