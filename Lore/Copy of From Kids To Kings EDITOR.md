**From Kids To Kings**

The light from the floating candles that hang in the halls fade and the crowd goes silent. Bright orbs are cast high above the rafters of the stage by the magical crew as the curtains draw to the wings. In the middle of the stage is an elvish woman in a kimono. Her hands crossed and hidden in the opposite sleeves. The beauty of her radiant red hair is only matched by her piercing blue eyes as they slowly show themselves. The group audibly gasps at her beauty. She raises her arms out of her kimono’s elegant sleeves before speaking to the heavens.

**“My name is Princess Infaldia. To many I am known as the daughter of the Mad Druid King. To others I am the catalyst of a great war that ravaged the mortal realm. To one, lowly mortal, I am known as their tender lover. . .”** The actress gives pause after having used her great gestures to emphasize each reveal. The crowd begins to murmur things after hearing the last one, then quietly hushed by other theater goers. **“Many will call me blameless; A child morphed by the delusional tales of old, power-hungry forest folk. Yet desire that was blind to the woes of others began a harvest of blood that will haunt the very fabric of Elustria. This isn’t just a tale of tragic love, but about the power of grief driven by a children’s bedtime story.”**

The crowd erupts into a roaring cheer as the stage lights fade. You watch as the beautiful actress slowly disappears as she stands motionless. You hear the darting of bare feet on the wooden tatami floor. The next scene that appears before you is one painted brightly, as if interpreting the child-like wonder of a dreamscape. You see a group of men dressed in rags appear stage right, taking in the bountiful land before them. A deep, booming voice reverberates through the auditorium. It narrates the events as they unfold through the actors on stage. 

**“This is the beginning of creation. The first of mortal men roamed this small, small patch of land we call home. In a vast world of deep waters, this was all they knew. As they explored the two islands, they were met with a singular truth: this world was not built for mortal men. Everywhere you’d turn, death loomed in mystical and unexpected ways.  Demons more vile and rampant, beasts whose gaze peered over parapets, and vengeful apparitions from beyond. But man persevered as they learned the land. Tilling the soil, raising homes from the earth, reaching great heights through their craftsmanship. However, there was a kind of beast that lay dormant for centuries while in plain sight. A beast so foul that they would one day show the mortal realm the meaning of doomsday. . .”**  The crowd’s silence is deafening as the narrator takes his pause. **“A creature known as the Druid.”**

Three new actors join the play from stage left. They are gangly caricatures, hiding stilts underneath long, baggy robes. The golden accents of their dark green clothes glisten in the light. They would appear to be angelic characters if not for the masks that they wear. Helmets made of gold-painted chainmail with a tree bark cover in the front. It covers everything but the actors’ mouths. Scary holes for their four eyes and a crown with tall horns on them. They approached the mortal men, reaching down with their long, prosthetic fingers from above. They tower the other actors on stage.

**“These tree-folk are a clever and powerful people. Living just out of eye shot within the forests of your home. For many years they guided us towards greener pastures with a very strict yet nurturing fist. They dug their claws deep as many of the old houses slowly grew to trust those of the Druid Nations. However we did not know of the tales that they told their litter. . .”** 

Lights out, pitter patter, lights on. It’s the inside of a communal tree house where many wood-masked children sit around a bonfire. One of the tall druids dances on his stilts while pretending to speak to the children. Behind him is a shadow puppet show displaying the narrator’s tale.

**“It is believed that many years ago, the Druids were all birthed by three individuals who could draw their energy through nature. These three Druids created all others and ruled together for centuries. Many don’t know that their history does mention a time of peace back then. They were said to have aided the first mortals before any house had formed.”** A gaggle of chuckles and scoffs echo in the theater as the narrator gives pause to digest. **“However, one of them was said to have grown increasingly frustrated with the older tribes of the west. So he one day decided to purge them all. A small village in Old Elustria, gone. Consumed by the forest in the blink of an eye. . .”**

**“Although it is said that the Druid Nations continued to do this for many centuries, some believe that the other two creators stopped his madness. That they saw the error of their ways and chose to side with the mortals after such a heavy transgression. . .  It was the advancement of mortal kind that managed to scare them into aid, although they told their children otherwise.”** 

**“They speak of three powerful children, all born within a decade of each other, being the reincarnations of these Druidic deities.”**  The crowd chuckles a little at that comment. **“They speak of these self-proclaimed prophets’ great deeds to come. Destined at birth to enact great change, for better or for worse. They acknowledge to their children that they might one day cause great evil… Imagine how that must shape a child’s instincts and desires. . . Many of their rulers were chosen based on their mystical capabilities, rather than their accomplishments or merits. Truly, the signs of an inept society. . . Of course there are no records of any of these prophets having done anything of significant note, save for one. This story involves a princess and her father. . .”**

Stage fades to black again as the crew quickly changes the set and the actors rush into the green rooms. When the lights finally come back up you see that on stage left there is a palace room with a view overlooking a great city. On stage right there is a small hut on the beach near a quaint fishing village. Two figures appear from either end of the stage. Princess Infaldia in her room. Combing her hair and looking longingly out the window. A fisherman in peasant garb, busy untangling his net for the day. 

**“Many centuries later, in the time of the Warring Old Houses, two key figures would meet unexpectedly. Two worlds apart. Vastly different upbringings that had no business intertwining. Princess Infaldia was the eldest daughter of King Jerosha, ruler of the Druid Nations. And a poor Tantakhan fisherman called Eel-Frat.”**  More actors join the stage, two from either side. From the way that they dress and behave, one can elude to the fact that these are the parents of the respective young adult lovers. Infaldia’s are dressed in similar greenish-gold robes with crowns on their heads. The poor fishermen in tattered brown and straw hats. Eel-Frat bowed to his parents and followed the silent instructions as they pointed every which way on their side of the stage. He would quickly go around picking up props and organizing them while the princess brushed off her father’s arm as he gently approached her.

**“My dearest daughter, I did not mean to fright. . .”** The king exclaimed while turning to the audience and rolling his eyes. **“I just came to see you on your birthday. A national holiday I shall soon make it. . . We have prepared a feast in your honor. . . What say you?”**

**“Is that all your love will buy me?”** The princess retorted while turning dramatically towards her parents. She leans up against the window sill. The parents react and become nervous. **“You keep me locked up in this castle and go mad if the wind blows too strongly through my hair. . . Why must you keep me here, dearest father?”**

**“As the youngest of the prophets, I carry a heavy burden. One thrusted upon me since birth. . . I have many political enemies due to the title that was bestowed upon me as a wee baby. Unfortunately, as my kin, you carry that burden. . .”**

**“BUT DADDY\!\!\!\!”**  The crowd bursts into laughter at the older princess’s tantrum. **“I want to go out and meet cute boys\!\!\!”** 

**“The daughter of the third prophet was bull-headed, and would not heed the warnings of her father. It’s hard to blame a child raised by such creatures. They are of a different world. Their customs are crude and lacking. Unlike her eventual lover, she had not a shred of responsibility. Such is the life of Druid royalty. . . She would eventually come to learn what it means to be part of the mortal world.”**  The stage fades to black. The next scene that appears before you is of a guard post in the middle of the woods; A Druid checkpoint. Princess Infaldia enters stage left, sneaking around and covering her face with a hood. **“ The princess left her birthday banquet early, using the cover of night to venture out of the woods. Trading the only world she had ever known for a taste of mortal men.”** 

**“My lady? . . .”** One of the guards at their post recognizes the princess as she decides how to go about her escape. She quickly turns away, only to realize that it's too late. She lets down her hair by unhooding herself. **“What are you doing out here at this hour of the morning? What of your banquet? . . .”**  
   
**“I am leaving, sir. Please do not make this difficult for me, lest I make the rest of your life so. . .”** 

**“We are in trying times, my lady. I do not wish to incur your wrath, nor that of your father. . . May the lady reconsider attending her banquet and speak to His Majesty about leaving the woods?”**

**“I see. . . you fear my father’s wrath more than my own. . . I guess you wish to see how the apple doesn’t fall far from the tree then. . .”**

**“Please do not despise me, my Lady. I am but a humble servant fulfilling my duty to the king-”**

**“THEN STEP ASIDE\!”** Her voice booms through the auditorium. The light orbs flicker above the now terrified Druid guard.

**“Your highness\! The mortal men are at war. . . It is dangerous out there. . .”**

**“Is that a no? . . . ”** The stage lights tint red and the guard falls to his knees.

**“Okay\! . . . Okay, my lady. . .”**

**“Good\!”** Her voice is chipper and the crowd dies laughing. The lights immediately go back to normal as the princess skips past the guard post and out of the woods. The stage fades to black. 

**“And so began Princess Infaldia’s journey into Elustria. Unaware of the warring houses, and the importance of her position.”**  When the light returns it is back to the last set. Half of the stage is Eel-Frat’s beach side hut, and the Druid palace bedroom. Eel-Frat is hard at work. Throwing his net out into the crowd as the princess is seen sneaking up from the greenery behind him. On the other side, King Jerosha is pacing the bedroom floor with two guards bowing to him and another military officer standing at their side. “**King Jerosha had declared a state of emergency after having lost track of his own daughter. Summoning his generals to Infaldia’s quarters.”** 

**“So, you willingly let her go then?”** The Druid King turns to face the guards. 

**“Please, Your Highness. We were threatened by her. . .”** The guard that let her go pleads.

**“I see. . . you fear my daughter’s wrath more than my own. . .”** The king takes out his katana, struggling with it for comedic effect. As he is unable to take it out, the guards giggle, prompting the King to cast a spell on them. They grab their throats and pretend to choke. Falling limp and silent on the stage floor. The king brushes his hands off on his robe and proceeds to pace again. **“Any word from our scouts?”**

**“Not about Princess Infaldia, Your Highness.”** The general bows before speaking this. On the other side of the stage, the princess has made contact with Eel-Frat. They are nervous at first but slowly seem to communicate. **“However, they have some more urgent news. . .”**

**“What is it, Usamea?”** 

**“The mortal world has not heeded our warning about the use of our beloved trees. . . They have been continuing on their war path with one another, and their need for materials is never satiated. . . What shall we do, Your Highness?”**

**“Where could she be? What could she be doing? . . .”** The crowd laughs at this as Princess Infaldia takes Eel-Fart by the hand and takes him into the hut. After a few seconds, the hut starts shaking violently. **“I just can’t bear to think of how scared she might be out there.”**

**“I’m sure she is more than capable of handling mortals, sir. She is your daughter after all. . .”**

Lights fade out once more. A spotlight falls on the two starstruck lovers as they make their way through the auditorium. The audience turns their head as they watch them prance and giggle while the narrator talks over a shadow puppet show going on backstage.

**“The starstruck lovers ran away from their homes and traveled Old Elustria. Spending what little waking moments they had together in inseparable passion. For many moons they had managed to avoid the clutches of battlefields and their barbaric warriors, all thanks to Eel-Frat; a man familiar with the times. . .”**

**“This country is more breathtaking than I could have ever dreamed. . .”** Exclaims the princess. The couple makes it back on stage as the shadow fighting wages on behind them. **“Our tomes do not do it justice. . . And your kind is way gentler than described in my books. . .”**

**“Please, Infaldia. I must rest. . . ”** He sits down on stage and prepares a camp for them. Infaldia sits and doesn’t lift a finger. **“We might have been able to make it to the Shilu Mountains had we not stopped so much for . . . a snack, as you say. . .”**

**“I enjoy savoring every bite. . .”** Infaldia scoots over to Eel-Frat and starts playing with his hair while he starts making a fire. **“What will we see next, my love?”**

**“I’ve heard that the coast line near Miolmorr Sea is gorgeous this time next year.”**

**“Correct me if I’m wrong, my love, but isn’t that closer to my home town? . . .”** Infladia waits for a response but Eel-Frat just lowers his head. Unable to respond to her. **“My love? . . .”**

**“I’ve gotten word that the Connecting Isles are a lot harder to cross now that the Imperiamotto have been pushed back by the Farfalleon. . .”** He turns and takes her by her hands. Looks deeply into her eyes. **“It’s not safe out here anymore, my love. . . I just thought maybe-”**

**“Maybe you would return me to my father\!”** She stands up abruptly and storms away a few feet. Pouting her cheeks and crossing her arms like a child. This makes the audience chuckle.

**“Infaldia, I beg of you\! We will have time to travel in the future. . .”** He walks over to her and hugs her from behind. As they embrace, a group of mortals in armor slowly sneak up on them from off stage. **“I fear for your safety in these trying times. . .”**

**“I cannot return, for I fear that I will never be able to see you again. . . My father is a petty man. I’m not sure you’ll make it out alive if you accompany me. . .”**

**“Then let that be my fate. . .”** They embrace one last time before the soldiers throw a net over them and surround them. As they yell for help, the lights and shadow puppets fade.

**“The young lovers were taken. Till this day no one knows which mortal clan took them, but what we do know is that they were swiftly executed. Travelers suspected as spies. Lovers suspected traitors. A love story cut short. Though their intentions were innocent, Princess Infaldia had not taken into account her father’s reaction to her passing.”**

Stage lights return to both worlds. The Druid bedroom and the shack on the beach. Eel-Frats parents are mourning the death of their son through prayer and practice. King Jerosha is stomping his feet as his wife weeps.

**“General Usamea\!\!\!\!”** The general quickly arrives at his side, followed by two heavily equipped soldiers.

**“Your Highness?”**

**“Prepare the war council\!\!\! We march on the capitals of the Farfalleon and the Imperiamotto at once\!”** 

**“Are you certain, your highness?”**

**“You dare ask such a question, Usamea\!\!?”** The lights flicker and go red for a moment before returning to normal.

**“To go to war now, after waiting so long. . . ”**

**“I cannot wait any longer. Our people are sick of letting these filthy mortals trample over our precious lands. . .”** He exclaims as a few more mortals come to pay their respects to Eel-Frat. **“Besides, they have tarnished the royal bloodline. They have soiled the name of the prophet. They are no longer deserving of our empathy\!”**

**“Although I agree that they have pushed boundaries, what will this mean for our people? To go to war now. . . The mortals are no longer what they once were-”**

**“They are MONKEYS compared to us\! . . . ”** The lights go red. **“They will not be able to stand against the might of the trees. Call forth the war council, Great General Usamea\! LET THE RAMPAGING FOREST LOOSE\!\!\!\!\!”**

As he utters these words, the red lights flicker in strobe like fashion. The general and his soldiers march on to the other side of the stage and begin attacking the mourning mortals.

**”The mortals had been blindsided by this act of betrayal from the ones that claimed to be a caring, nurturing people. This act of wild violence was later called the Rampaging Forest Campaign. It decimated most of the tamed countryside we now know as Palomeras. Many small clans had been engulfed by this bloody forest, while powerful ones such as the Farfalleon and Imperiamotto had almost been whipped out. Some say that it lasted weeks, months even, but the truth is that it only lasted a single day.”**  You notice that the crowd is shocked by this fact. The strobe lights fade back to a light blue hue as the soldiers flee offstage, leaving behind the bodies of all the mortals. The king lay at his daughter’s bedside, weeping along with his wife. **“Till this day, no one knows what possessed the Mad Druid King to stop. Some say it was grief, others say that the mortal clans fought back. But one thing we do know for sure is that they still lurk in the woods of your home. . . Be wary, lest you let a beast in by mistake. . .”**

The lights fade and when they come back on the cast is on stage. In line, ready to give a bow. The crowd cheers, especially for Princess Infaldia. They exit stage right and left as the curtains draw and the house lights fade back in.

You are expecting to see more short plays after a quick intermission when suddenly you notice a number of uniformed guards enter the rows of the auditorium. One of them stands on stage. They all wear beige-brown uniforms with little to no armor plating. They wear white boots and gloves, have a black plague-doctor masks on, and a sigil on their left breast. A book inside of a hexagon.

**“Unfortunately, tonight’s evening entertainment has concluded.”** The uniformed man on stage yells. The crowd starts to murmur amongst themselves, but no one seems to want to make a fuss. **“Please exit in an orderly fashion, thank you. . .”**
